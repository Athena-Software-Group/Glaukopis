# Journal shortlist for Glaukopis and FAU CS PhD planning

**Checked:** September 5, 2026. This is a publication-strategy assessment, not a promise of journal acceptance, a guaranteed review duration, or a ruling by the FAU dissertation committee. Publisher metrics can change. An initial editorial decision is not the same as a completed external review.

## Recommendation

**Speed-first:** IEEE Access is the most defensible first target among the journals investigated, after the audit gates in the revision memorandum are closed. It explicitly accommodates applied engineering work and negative findings, and provides the clearest publisher-stated rapid-review process. It is a broad engineering journal, not an elite specialist security Transactions title.

**Specialist-security-first:** Computers & Security is the strongest primary specialist fit in this shortlist, in my assessment. Journal of Information Security and Applications is a good applied-security alternative. Their current reviewed-decision and acceptance durations could not be verified from accessible publisher dashboards, so they are not represented as four-to-six-week options.

Submit to one venue at a time. Obtain the advisor/committee's written approval for the intended title before paying fees or committing a substantial part of the graduation timeline.

The [FAU Computer Science PhD degree requirements](https://www.fau.edu/engineering/eecs/graduate/phd/computer-science/degree-reqs/) state that a candidate is expected to have at least one paper **published or accepted for publication** in a fully refereed conference or journal before graduation. Thus, acceptance rather than a final issue date can satisfy the wording of the public requirement. The inspected page does not provide a specific approved-journal list, minimum Journal Impact Factor, or quartile threshold. ### Why IEEE Access is the practical first choice

The [scope page](https://ieeeaccess.ieee.org/about/) expressly includes applied engineering, measurement, practical solutions, and negative results. That is a better match for the defensible version of Glaukopis than a claim of a novel training algorithm. Its [rapid-review page](https://ieeeaccess.ieee.org/about/rapid-peer-review/) gives actual publication-process targets; this is stronger evidence of speed than a generic low median first-decision statistic.

The [submission guidelines](https://ieeeaccess.ieee.org/authors/submission-guidelines/) require an IEEE Access template and biographies, list all authors on source and PDF, prohibit simultaneous submission, and require disclosure of AI-generated text. Consequently, it is an author-identified journal route rather than a workaround that attempts to hide a publicly named project. Paying an APC is not payment for acceptance or favorable reviews.

### Why not simply choose the highest impact factor?

TIFS would be a more demanding prestige target for the current experimental claim. A committee may value that specialist standing, but trying it first could work against the user's speed objective. Conversely, Access has a practical publishing process but should not be described as equivalent in prestige to every IEEE Transactions journal. This trade-off should be resolved explicitly with the advisor, not concealed behind citation metrics.

International Journal of Information Security is a particularly useful warning: **nine days to an initial decision and 175 days in the review pipeline can both be true**. A journal selected only from the smaller number would create a misleading schedule.
